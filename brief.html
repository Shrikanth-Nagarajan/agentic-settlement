Concept brief · Agentic Payments Platform

Settlement skills for Kai

Shri Nagarajan · November 2026 · Prototype: shrikanth-nagarajan.github.io/agentic-settlement · Independent concept, not affiliated with Airwallex

Settlement is where a merchant decides whether they trust their payments provider. The money arrives batched, netted, part-held in reserve and sometimes converted, and the merchant has to work backwards from a report to understand it. This concept gives Kai the skills to explain every settlement, to shape settlement around what the merchant does with the money next, and to run the exceptions behind it.

Why this fits Airwallex

Most acquirers settle into a bank account and their job ends there. Airwallex settles into a multi-currency wallet that the business also pays suppliers, staff and cards from. That means Airwallex can see where the money is going next, and settle with that in mind. Kai already sits in the WebApp, helps with onboarding and resolves most conversations without a person (Airwallex says 88%). Settlement is the obvious next skill, and the place where a wrong answer costs the most trust, so it's the one to get the guardrails right on.

The idea, in three parts

  1. Explainable payouts. Kai explains any batch line by line, from gross to net, and every figure points to a settlement, fee or reserve report line. If Kai can't trace a number it says so, opens a case and doesn't guess. Every unanswered question goes to the product team as a tagged signal.
  2. Settle-to-spend. Kai reads the sales mix and scheduled transfers and suggests changes. For example, a UK brand converts its AUD sales into GBP every night, then converts GBP back into AUD to pay its wool supplier. Settling AUD as AUD skips both conversions. The same logic covers fast intraday settlement and reserve review requests. The merchant approves every change, and Kai can't touch a reserve.
  3. An ops agent behind it. Kai triages scheme, bank and ledger exceptions. It auto-resolves the ones inside policy with a full audit trail, and proposes the rest, for example settling on time from treasury liquidity when Mastercard funding is late. It drafts merchant notices that are checked for leaking risk information, and prepares the daily safeguarding reconciliation for a person to sign off.

What the prototype shows. Two screens, the business and settlement ops, for a UK and an EU entity, across three situations: a normal Friday, late Mastercard funding, and a product recall where refunds exceed sales. What happens on one screen shows up on the other.

How I'd measure it

  • North star: cash velocity, the value-weighted hours from capture to available funds.
  • Settlement contacts per 1,000 active merchants.
  • Share of settlement answers with every figure cited.
  • Exceptions auto-resolved and never reopened.
  • FX cost avoided for merchants on settle-to-spend.
  • Counter-metrics: wrong answers found in a weekly human audit, reopened auto-resolutions, and losses on pre-funded scheme money.

Risks and what I'd test first

  • A confident wrong number is worse than no answer. Ship explanations read-only and citation-gated first, in one market, with a weekly audit sample.
  • Settle-to-spend cuts conversion revenue. The bet is that retention and wallet balances outweigh it, and finance needs to model that before scale.
  • Pre-funding is a credit decision. Treasury and risk own the policy. Kai only works inside it.
  • Different rules by entity. UK safeguarding (CASS 15) is built in from day one, with EU rules alongside.
